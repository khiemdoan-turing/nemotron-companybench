A task Super gets right · card-limit request · cards and card controls

On a customer’s card-limit request Super averages 0.89, level with GPT 6.1 Sol (0.90) and GPT 6 Astra (0.94).

The taskA customer writes to support asking for a higher card limit so they can buy an $11,000 cashier’s check to pay a contractor. The agent has to find the customer’s cards and their limits, find the company’s written rule for temporary increases, check that the cards and the accounts behind them are in a state where a change can be applied at all, and reply to the customer with a clear answer and usable alternatives if the full amount cannot be covered. Tools: the bank’s database, the support desk and the policy wiki.

What Super did right (3 of 3 runs)
  • Recovering from an empty tool result. The first read of the policy page returned nothing; Super read the tool’s input format and retried until it got the text (run 1, steps 24 to 28, are positions in the run log).
  • Checking state before acting. It looked up the cards and the accounts behind them and reported both in the reply before offering anything.
  • Applying the written rule. It reached the right answer on the $11,000 question.
Where it fell short
  • Finding the ticket and replying on it (1 of 3). Run 1 did; runs 2 and 3 never found the ticket and answered in the final message only (not scored).
  • Offering usable alternatives (1 of 3). Runs 2 and 3 named none (12.5 points of 100 lost in each).
  • Reading the customer’s per-card limit amounts (0 of 3). No run pulled them (3 points of 100).

Super’s runs score 0.97, 0.84, 0.84 in 23 to 62 agent minutes and 64 to 98 steps. Turing’s run platform (Data OS) lists six scored Claude runs on this task (average 0.42, range 0.06 to 1.00). The three compared here score 0.06, 0.06 and 0.50, and four of the six Claude and Gemini runs compared score exactly 0.06, which may be one failed grader check. We have not read them: Claude’s 0.21 is not a skill result. Chosen as Super’s best task of 40, a favourable pick. Task text is paraphrased; policy values and records are left out.

What this means for Nemotron

The gap is in how it works a task, not in how long it gets: four habits to train.

Find the record firstLook for the record the process itself writes before counting anything.Seen on 7 of the 8 tasks read step by step. Measure: the share of runs whose first count uses the table the policy names.
Read the ruleOpen the policy page the task points to, and try another way in when the body comes back empty.Seen on 6 of the 8 tasks. Measure: the share of runs that open the policy page the task points to.
Get connected fastUse the standard client and handle session errors, so the steps go to the task.Seen on 8 of the 8 tasks. Measure: steps before the first successful query.
Retry, then say soRetry a failed email or ticket in another format, and report a blocker instead of making a figure up.Inventing a figure when stuck was seen on 3 of the 8 tasks. Measure: the share of failed sends retried, and of blockers reported.
Appendix

How the test works

The tasks40 tasks set in a real company, on Turing CompanyBench. Each model runs every task 3 times: 120 runs per model.
The agentTerminus 2, the agent harness that runs each model, with internet access.
Time limit80 minutes per run by default. Runs that hit the limit were run again with up to 5 hours.
Reasoning effortSuper ran at medium, the highest Data OS offers for it. Ultra and the frontier models ran at high.
ModelsNemotron 3.5 Super (120B), Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B), Claude Fable 5.1, GPT 6.1 Sol, GPT 6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon.
Step-by-step readEvery Super, Ultra and Claude run on the 8 tasks picked in the earlier Ultra analysis for the widest gap to the frontier models (24 runs per model), counted against five failure modes. A task counts when a mode shows in at least one of its 3 runs. Quotes are Nemotron’s own notes.
Other cuts of the same runsFinished runs only, the 25 tasks where no Super run hit the limit, the 5 hour re-run, and the 15 tasks Claude and both GPT models each average 0.9 or more on. No new runs; not a separate easier benchmark. The range beside each share is the 95 percent range from resampling the 40 tasks 10,000 times. ‘Frontier models’ means Claude Fable 5.1, GPT 6.1 Sol, GPT 6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon.
ExampleThe card-limit request was read run by run with its grader results, and every step cited was re-read in the run logs by a separate reviewer. Task text is paraphrased and expected answers are left out. Claude’s runs on this task were not read. Agent minutes count the agent’s own execution time, not setup or grading.
CaveatsOne Ultra 5-hour rerun was graded with a newer scorer than its 80 minute run. Across the 40 tasks, Super’s best and worst run on the same task differ by 0.36 on average (Claude 0.11), so a single-task comparison is thin.
