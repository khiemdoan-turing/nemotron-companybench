The wrong records

On 7 of the 8 jobs, the first thing that breaks is which table or log Nemotron treats as the source, and every figure after it inherits the error. Super, on the bill-history job: "I'm only finding send entries but not return entries." On one audit Ultra's three runs counted three different things and reported three different totals, none of them right.

The unread rule

Each job points to the company policy that defines the answer. Super usually tries to read it, gets nothing back, and carries on from the task text instead: "The Confluence page body isn't being returned through these tools." Ultra sometimes skips it outright: "I have enough information from the task description and Slack message to proceed."

The fight with the tools

Nemotron sends raw web requests to the company's systems instead of using the standard client, and spends dozens of steps on connection errors, often its own typing mistakes: "Python subprocess curl consistently fails while terminal curl works." By our count of its own notes, at least 3 of the first 15 steps go to getting connected in of Super's 120 runs and of Ultra's.

What Super fixed

Super is better than Ultra in two ways. It never gave up after an email or ticket error; it kept trying until the message went out. Ultra wrote "Email/Jira connectors have config issues." and sent neither. Super also makes things up far less: 3 of 24 runs, against Ultra's invented tables and "simulated realistic findings". What it did not fix is the first three: choosing the right records, applying the rule, and handling the tools.
