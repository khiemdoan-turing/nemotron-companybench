What happened in each of the 120 runs (80 minute limit)

Read step by step, both Nemotron models lose tasks the same way: wrong records, unread rules, and a long fight with the tools.

We read every Super, Ultra and Claude run line by line on the 8 tasks picked in the earlier Ultra analysis for the widest gap to the frontier models, the same way for all three. On these 8 tasks Claude gets full marks on 21 of 24 runs, Super on 1 and Ultra on none. These 8 are the widest gaps, not a random sample. Quotes are Nemotron's own notes from its run logs.

The wrong records

On 7 of the 8 tasks, the first thing that breaks is which table or log Nemotron treats as the source, and every figure after it inherits the error. Super, on the bill-history task: "I'm only finding send entries but not return entries." On one audit Ultra's three runs counted three different things and reported three different totals, none of them right.

The unread rule

Each task points to the company policy that defines the answer. Super usually tries to read it, gets nothing back, and carries on from the task text instead: "The Confluence page body isn't being returned through these tools." Ultra sometimes skips it outright: "I have enough information from the task description and Slack message to proceed."

The fight with the tools

Nemotron sends raw web requests to the company's systems instead of using the standard client, and spends dozens of steps on connection errors, often its own typing mistakes: "Python subprocess curl consistently fails while terminal curl works." By our count of its own notes, at least 3 of the first 15 steps go to getting connected in of Super's 120 runs and of Ultra's.

What Super fixed

Super is better than Ultra in two ways. It never gave up after an email or ticket error; it kept trying until the message went out. Ultra wrote "Email/Jira connectors have config issues." and sent neither. Super also makes things up far less: on 3 of the 8 tasks, against 8 for Ultra, whose runs invent tables and "simulated realistic findings". What it did not fix is the first three: choosing the right records, applying the rule, and handling the tools.

Super scores 0.5 or more on 42 of its 120 runs, up from 13 for Ultra, and on its best task it is level with GPT.

Super gets 42 of its 120 runs to 0.5 or more, against 13 for Ultra. Each card has its number next to it, from the same 120 runs per model as the comparison above.

A clear step up from Ultra

Average 0.40 against 0.25, and a higher average on 30 of the 40 tasks. 42 of 120 runs score 0.5 or more (Ultra 13 of 120) and 24 score 0.7 or more (Ultra 4). Runs that finish and still score zero: Super 3, Ultra 12 (of 120 runs each). Counting timeouts, Super scores exactly zero on 21 runs, 18 of them timeouts; Ultra on 17, 5 of them timeouts.

Level with GPT on its best task

On the card-limit request Super averages 0.89, against 0.90 for GPT 6.1 Sol and 0.94 for GPT 6 Astra. Its average is above Claude’s on 3 of the 40 tasks: the card-limit request (Claude’s 0.21 on it is unexplained; we did not read those runs), the failed bill-payment diagnosis (Claude 0.00) and new-account beneficiary inheritance (0.49 against 0.45).

It did not stop at an email or ticket error

In the 3 runs where the notes record an email or ticket failure, Super kept retrying: in 2 the message went out, in 1 the clock ran out first. Across its 24 runs on 8 tasks read step by step it never stopped at the first error; Ultra did in 12 of 24. The 8 tasks were picked in the earlier Ultra analysis for the widest gap to the frontier models and are not a sample.

A timed-out task can pass on a second try

On the person-to-person bill history audit the three 80 minute runs hit the limit (0.25, 0.00, 0.00): two while fighting tool call problems, one while retrying an email. The three re-runs scored 1.00, 0.94, 0.34 and took 23 to 34 minutes each, well inside that limit.
